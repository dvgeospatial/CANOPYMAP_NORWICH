Norwich Shade Map Guide

How to Use Norwich Shade Map

  1. Change the Time of Day: Click Time, then drag the bottom slider left or right (or scroll with your mouse wheel) to watch tree and building shadows move throughout the day.
  2. Change the Season: Click Date and slide through the months to see how shadows shift between summer and winter.
  3. Daylight Hours: The bright yellow bar on the time slider shows sunrise to sunset.
  4. Return to Live: Click Now at any time to jump straight back to current real-time shade.
  5. Explore Norwich: Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, and right-click & drag (or use two fingers on touch screens) to tilt and rotate the 3D map.

What is the Norwich Shade Map for?

To explore the dynamic of urban shade around Norwich, Wise Frog have developed the Norwich Shade Map. This 3D interactive web map visualises how shadows cast by the built environment and tree canopies shift throughout daylight hours for every day of the year.

The tree canopy layer is a Wise Frog product named canopyMap and is derived from Environment Agency LiDAR data. This is combined with extruded OpenStreetMap building footprints and solar angles to model the shade.

The Norwich Shade Map has potential uses in urban planning and public health:

  • Targeting "shade deserts": Pinpointing streets, school routes, and public spaces lacking sun protection to prioritise targeted tree planting.
  • Assessing climate equity: Layering shade availability with demographic, age, and health vulnerability data to ensure fair access to cooler outdoor spaces.
  • Informing urban design for active travel: Shaded pedestrian walkways and bike lanes are more attractive and comfortable to use during the summer months.
  • Wildlife benefits: Urban shade benefits wildlife; it creates cooler, less dry microhabitats that help wildlife avoid heat stress, rest, forage and move through cities during hot weather.

Understanding the Sun Data

Next to the time controls, you will see two numbers showing the sun’s exact position:

  • Height in the Sky (∡ e.g., 54.7°): This is the sun's elevation above the horizon. 0° means the sun is right on the horizon (sunrise or sunset). Higher values mean the sun is high overhead, creating shorter, tighter shadows. Values below 0° mean it is night time.
  • Compass Direction (e.g., 195.0° SSW): This tells you where the sun is coming from in the sky (its compass heading). Shadows always stretch directly in the opposite direction (for example, a sun in the South creates shadows pointing North).
